Verifying agreements on compute use using trusted networking hardware

A simple explanation of how we capture, scrub and verify inference traffic at a datacenter north‑south link.

Abstract

As AI becomes more powerful, it is increasingly important that we are able to verify the adherence of datacenters to international agreements on compute use. Much of the research in this area focuses on compliance – verifying that known data centre workloads abide by certain regulations or guidelines (e.g., that models cannot be trained on copyrighted data, or that inference inputs should not contain prompts asking models how to build a bioweapon).

Our proposal targets the datacenter’s north‑south uplink—the communication chokepoint between the cluster and the outside world. An evidence capture device ("border patrol") at this link hashes and timestamps every packet payload (evidence capture) while actively scrubbing covert channels so traffic is reproducible. The prover retains plaintext internally; the verifier later requests samples and replays the same computation to compare hashes. This shifts trust from supply chains to observable physics, giving regulators or treaty partners a practical way to check comprehensive compute use without direct access to sensitive data.

Our evidence capture device: Border patrol

Every packet in or out of the datacenter must pass through our evidence capture device, so it can both record evidence and remove covert channels. We illustrate below what this would look like.

Packet flow, step by step

1) Inference produces a payload
The datacenter runs a model and produces output tokens. Inside the datacenter, the payload is in plaintext (e.g., JSON chunks in a streaming API).
2) Border patrol records evidence
For each packet, the device hashes the payload and adds a timestamp. Only hashes + timestamps are sent to the verifier. The plaintext stays inside the datacenter.
3) Active warden scrubs side‑channels
Volatile fields (header IDs, checksums, timing quirks) are normalized so the packet stream is reproducible. This blocks steganography and timing channels.
4) Packets exit over the north‑south link
The sanitized packets go out to users. The verifier can later pick any hash and ask the provider to show the matching plaintext for replay verification.

The two core tasks the border patrol device performs

Evidence capture

For every packet: compute hash(payload) + timestamp and forward those to a verifier. The verifier doesn’t see the payload, but can later check that the provider’s declared traffic matches the log.

Active warden (scrubbing)

Normalize headers and timing so the traffic is reproducible. This closes off covert channels that could hide undeclared computation.

Prover–verifier replay check: Why traffic reproducibility matters

The prover and verifier both execute the same declared workload. They each compute hashes of the packet stream after scrubbing. If the hashes match, the verifier is confident the prover did not run undeclared workloads outside the captured traffic.

Hash + timestamp are computed over the payload stream and sent to verifier.

The warden removes run‑to‑run noise in headers/timing, turning a non-reproducible packet stream into a reproducible one.

How the simulation maps to the real device

The warden codebase is a concrete toy version of this flow:

Server (datacenter)

Precomputes Ethernet frames from a JSON payload and sends them after a UDP trigger. This is the “deterministic inference output”.

Client (user)

Sends the trigger, receives packets, and computes a final hash. Optional steganography mode adds a hidden packet to demonstrate detection.

Analyzer and warden

Normalizes volatile fields (IP ID, checksums) and compares the two captures. If traffic matches after scrubbing, it passes.

    \             v

       ------> compare

Glossary

North‑south link = the uplink between the datacenter and the public internet.
Packet = a small chunk of data sent over the network.
Hash = a short, fixed‑size fingerprint of data; changing any byte changes the hash.
Warden = a device that removes “extra degrees of freedom” in network traffic.
Replay verification = re‑run the computation later and check the hashes match.
